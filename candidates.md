# Candidates — The One-Liner Groan-Off

Scored 1–5 against the five judge criteria, in order:
1. Is a one-line dad joke (pun/twist a dad would tell out loud)
2. Punchline lands fast and clean, setup + turn inside the line, no wasted words
3. Fresh, not heard a hundred times
4. Tight and rhythmic, easy to say out loud and pass on
5. Groan earned: silly enough to laugh at, sharp enough to repeat

Recycled classics deliberately avoided: "Hi Hungry, I'm Dad", orange-you-glad,
"facial hair grew on me", "waist of time", "stairs are always up to something".

## Top 3 — pairwise comparison

Finalists: #12 calendar / past dates (24), #11 lawn / cutting back (23),
#5 dad-abase (21, wins the 21-point tie on C3 freshness over #8 and #10).

- **#12 vs #11:** both parse instantly with the punchline at the end, but
  "past dates" is a single-word double meaning (calendar dates / romantic
  dates) the judge has not seen, while "cutting back" is a familiar
  double-meaning shape. #12 wins on C3 and C4 (shorter, more rhythmic).
- **#12 vs #5:** "dad-abase" is clever but needs a beat to decode
  (database → dad-abase) and is self-referential; #12's pun lands the moment
  "dates" is heard. #12 wins on C2 and C4.
- **#11 vs #5:** the lawn line parses instantly and reads aloud cleanly;
  the dad-abase line wins on raw novelty but loses on delivery. #11 wins.

Winner: **#12 — "My calendar and I broke up — it kept reminding me of all my past dates."**

## Try to beat the winner — 3 variations

- V1: "My calendar dumped me — it couldn't stop bringing up my past dates." (67)
  "Dumped" is more vivid than "broke up", and "bringing up" adds a second
  layer (mentioning the past / the calendar literally surfacing reminders).
  Tighter, punchier, punchline still at the end. **Clearly stronger — kept.**
- V2: "I broke up with my calendar — it kept reminding me of all my ex-dates." (74)
  "Ex-dates" is a fresh coinage but slightly forced; weaker rhythm than V1.
- V3: "My calendar and I are done — it never stops reminding me of my past dates." (76)
  "Are done" is flatter than "dumped me" and the turn loses snap.

## Final line (67 characters)

My calendar dumped me — it couldn't stop bringing up my past dates.
